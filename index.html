1 · The problem

A visitor standsbefore a statueand learns nothing.

No plaque, or a plaque in a language they can't read. They photograph it, search, and walk on.

1 · Evidence claims, evidence and assumptions kept apart

650,000 statues andmemorials. Almost nonecan introduce themselves.

  • Secondary · the scale88,691 statues, 72,042 monuments and 518,078 memorials are mapped on OpenStreetMap (taginfo, Oct 2026). 1.4 billion international tourist arrivals in 2024, 747 million in Europe (UN Tourism Barometer, Jan 2025).
  • Primary · the Skopje field countWalking all 122 statues of Skopje for the prototype: most have no explanatory plaque, and the few that exist are in the local language only.
  • Behaviour · the prototypeWorking prototype (Hacktoberfest 2026, Skopje): 122 statues, photo recognition for 102, 3D/AR, seven UI languages, offline. Tested on phones and jury.
  • Assumption · still to testThat visitors will tap or scan when a plaque invites them, and that cities will pay for it. Both go into the validation plan.

2 · The innovation claim in one sentence

What is actually new.

For city visitors who struggle to learn who a statue shows and why, TapTales differs from Talking Statues and Smartify by generating a complete guide for every statue of any city from open data, delivered by a physical tap or an on-device photo match, creating a city-wide guide in weeks instead of 35 hand-picked statues in years, free for the visitor and cheaper than a custom app for the city.

2 · Type and level of novelty

Each part exists.The combination,city-wide, does not.

We claim the combination and the process, never the parts.

  • Product / service innovationa guide that covers every statue of a city, with tap, photo, voice, 3D/AR and a collection
  • Process innovationan open pipeline (OpenStreetMap + Wikidata + Commons + Wikipedia) that generates a city in weeks, not months of curation
  • Business-model innovationfree for visitors; cities pay a per-city onboarding and licence instead of building their own app
  • Level: new to the world for the combinationincremental for every single part. Degree of change: a capability leap for small and mid-sized cities that could never afford a guide

3 · The product

Tap the plaque,or take a photo.

Story in your language, a voice, 3D you can place in front of you, and the statue joins your collection.

  • TapNFC plaque opens the statue, no app
  • Listenvoice guide, seven languages
  • See it in 3DAR for the headline statues
  • Collectonly a tap or a photo counts

3 · Value baseline → target, measured in the pilot

Why the differencematters.

For visitors: time and quality. For cities: cost, coverage and data they never had.

  • Time to “who is this?”2–3 min searching→< 5 s
  • Statues covered per city~35 (Talking Statues)→every statue
  • Cost to the city per statuecustom app €30k+→≈ €60 + licence
  • Languages at the statue1 (the plaque)→7
  • Visit data for the citynone→taps per statue, monthly

4 · Technical feasibility only the components that matter to novelty

A web app, a plaque and an open pipeline.

  1. Visitorany phone made after 2018 reads NFC; nothing to install
  2. NFC plaque / camerapassive sticker with a link; or a photo when there is no plaque
  3. PWA in the browserReact + TypeScript; installs from the browser; map, 3D, voice, collection; works offline
  4. On-device recognitionDINOv2 embeddings compared on the phone against the city's reference set; no server, no upload
  5. Open data pipelineOSM + Wikidata + Commons + Wikipedia → statues, facts, photos, stories for any city; reviewed by a human
  6. Static hosting + ARCDN for content and 3D models; AR Quick Look / WebXR; city permit for plaques

where the novelty livesstandard componentsexternalHardest uncertainty: recognition accuracy outdoors in bad light, and whether the pipeline holds in cities with thin Wikidata coverage.

Visitors use it free. Cities pay per city.

Key partnersOpenStreetMap and Wikimedia communities · universities in each pilot city (students collect photos) · plaque manufacturer · 3D-scanning studio · city heritage offices
Key activitiesonboarding cities from open data · plaque design and installation · content review and translation · sales to cities
Value propositionFor cities: every statue explained in seven languages, in weeks, for the price of plaques and a licence, with visit data, no app to build. For visitors: tap or snap, hear the story, free, no install, offline.
Customer relationshipspilot together · annual report of taps and routes · self-serve content corrections
Customer segmentsPay: cities, municipalities, tourism boards, park and heritage authorities. Use: foreign and domestic tourists, residents, schools.

Risks each with a specific response

What can go wrong, and what we do then.

  • Tech · recognition fails in bad lightthe plaque is primary, recognition is the fallback; more reference photos per statue; show three candidates to confirm
  • Market · cities don't buy after the pilotpilot with the city from day one; licence priced below a custom app; sponsored walks and heritage grants as a second revenue line
  • Legal · permits and content rightspermit before plaques are ordered; only open-licensed data (ODbL, CC0, CC BY-SA) with attribution; non-invasive stick-on plaques
  • Team · a student foundermentoring from FINKI and the Base42 incubator; accounting and legal outsourced; quarterly plan and budget review
  • Partner · pipeline thin outside Europebenchmark first (M1); city-editor mode so local volunteers and universities fill the gaps
  • Responsible ICTphotos processed on the phone and never uploaded; no location stored; anonymous counts only; voice guide for visually impaired visitors; passive plaques, no batteries, no recognition servers

IP searched before we claimed

No patent coversthe whole. We protectthe name, not the idea.

Google Patents, WIPO and product search, October 2026.

  • Prior art foundTalking Statues (2013) is published practice, so “NFC plaque on a statue” is nobody's to patent. Google US 8,810,599 / US 8,890,896 claim server-side image matching for device positioning; Universal US 12,705,994 needs a beacon-triggered camera and LLM text. We do none of these.
  • Freedom to operateno EP or WO family found for NFC + heritage + 3D; Chinese scenic-spot patents (CN104298965A, CN109036228A) are territorial and don't combine the parts
  • What we protecttrademark “TapTales” and the column mark (national office, then Madrid system); copyright on stories, voice scripts and 3D models
  • What we opencore app under an open licence (a selling point to cities: no vendor lock-in); data reused under ODbL / CC with attribution
